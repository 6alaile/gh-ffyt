# Matchday 1 — UCL 2026/27 Long-Form Recap Script

*(Draft — conversational, first-person commentary voice. Timestamps and stats verified against match reports; trim/adjust pacing in the edit.)*

---

Right, the Champions League is back, and — as always — I've got way too much to say about it. This is genuinely one of my favourite things to cover all season. It's not a deep dive, it's not an explainer, it's just... the competition doing what it does, week one, and me reacting to it in real time. So let's not waste any time. Matches were spread across the usual three days, so let's go day by day, starting with Tuesday.

## Tuesday

The headline fixture was obviously Real Madrid against Inter. So much going on in this one before a ball was even kicked — Mourinho back at the club he left Inter to go and manage in the first place, now lining up against the manager who inherited his old job. Mbappé, a boyhood Milanista, on the opposite side of the pitch. Plenty of storylines.

And honestly? Tactically, I thought Inter were the better side for long stretches. Chivu set them up really well — they had Madrid chasing shadows in periods of this game. But they beat themselves. Real's opener came from a straightforward turnover just outside Inter's box that Mbappé finished with one of his first touches of the night — his 71st Champions League goal, by the way, which pulls him level with Raúl on the all-time scoring list. Remarkable number for a player his age.

The second was even more avoidable. Inter's keeper got caught in possession trying to play out from the back, Valverde pounced, and it's 2-0 before the half-hour mark. Two gifts, basically. And that's the story of the whole match — Inter had the ball, had the territory, had twenty-plus shots by full time, but individual mistakes cost them the game. Courtois had to be excellent to protect the lead too — a string of saves, including a really good one to deny a deflected strike that could've made it interesting much earlier.

Inter did pull one back late through a substitute finishing off a low cross, but it was too little too late. 2-1 final score, and Mourinho gets a winning start against his old club. I'll say this for Inter, though — I don't think this is a "worry" performance. Take away the two individual errors and this was a really composed European away display. Early season, I care more about how a team is playing than what the scoreline says, and Inter looked like a team that's going to be fine.

Elsewhere on Tuesday — and speaking of players on ridiculous scoring streaks — Haaland just continues to be absurd. Two goals against Porto, ice-cold composure for both, and the second one mattered because Porto were genuinely threatening a way back into that game in the second half. That takes him to fifty-nine goals in fifty-nine Champions League appearances. A goal a game. Every single time he steps on this stage. City won 2-0 and didn't really need to get out of second gear.

Also enjoyed watching Real Betis get a winner in France — a last-minute strike settling a five-goal match away at Lille. Nice story continuing there for a player who's been in electric form domestically too. Dortmund needed a grandstand finish of their own, coming from behind late on to edge Villarreal 3-2 — basically every goal in that game arrived after the hour mark. And Aston Villa won away at Club Brugge, which is starting to feel like a proper little rivalry after they met in the knockouts a couple of seasons back. AEK Athens got their campaign up and running too, edging past LASK 1-0 in Athens.

## Wednesday

Barcelona. My word. Not exactly a shock that they'd win this one, but the manner of it was something else. Raphinha opened the scoring inside three minutes — turned two defenders inside out in the box and finished with zero fuss — and then got a second in the second half off a gorgeous Pedri through-ball. Adeyemi, one of the new faces, curled in an absolute worldie from outside the area for the second goal of the night. Yamal then found the net himself with a free-kick that snuck through the wall, and a header off the bench rounded it out to make it five. Feyenoord got a consolation late on, but this was one-way traffic from minute three onward. Five goals, and Barcelona's front line already looks terrifyingly settled.

PSG were similarly ruthless at home to Slovan Bratislava, and it's worth flagging the sub-plot here — Slovan are managed by Yaya Touré, who becomes the first African coach in the history of this competition. Brutal draw for a historic first game, because PSG were relentless. Dembélé got the ball rolling with two goals inside the first twenty-five minutes, and then it turned into the Ferran Torres show — hat-trick on his Champions League debut for the club, one of which was a lovely curled effort from the edge of the box. Slovan pulled one back but PSG added a sixth late through Fabián Ruiz. 6-1, and about as emphatic a defence-of-the-title opener as you'll see.

Liverpool hosted Atlético at Anfield, and this had a bit of everything. Atlético took the lead on the counter — and it's becoming a running joke at this point how often this particular Atlético player seems to have Liverpool's number specifically at Anfield. Florian Wirtz was excellent for large parts of this one, creating chance after chance, and eventually got his reward with a scruffy finish just before half-time to level things up. Then Alexis Mac Allister thumped one in from distance in the second half for what turned out to be the winner. 2-1 Liverpool, and genuinely one of Wirtz's best performances since arriving — the goal involvement will come, but the quality of the play was there for everyone to see.

Arsenal had to graft for their win in Naples. Napoli sat in for most of the match, offering very little going forward, and it took a really well-struck effort from Ødegaard — curled in off the inside of the post — to actually break them down. Once Arsenal were ahead, Napoli finally showed some ambition, but by then it was too late. 1-0 away in one of the toughest atmospheres in Europe is a good result to bank.

A couple of smaller stories worth mentioning from Wednesday too — Stuttgart put three goals past Viking inside a frantic opening half-hour, and Sporting beat Galatasaray 3-1, with a penalty from Luis Suárez doing a lot of the damage before a well-worked set piece sealed it — extra spice given a familiar face came off the bench against the club that raised him.

## Thursday

Thursday was the calmer of the three days, but there were still a couple of things worth flagging. Manchester United took care of business away at Sabah without much fuss, 4-0, and Bayern were similarly dominant, putting five past Bodø/Glimt with goals spread across the squad — genuinely, if you're looking for a dark-horse pick to go all the way this season, I wouldn't be shocked if it's them on this evidence.

Fenerbahçe and Roma played out a draw that turned into one of the stranger post-match stories of the week — never mind the football, the manager situation there is a whole separate conversation for another day. On the pitch, a good late equalising header settled for a share of the points.

PSV and Shakhtar drew 1-1, and I'll just say — for the Americans watching, yes, that was indeed an American on the scoresheet for PSV, so he's doing just fine over there.

But the team that actually jumped out at me from Thursday's card was Como. A 4-1 win away at Leipzig in their first ever European away fixture, and they didn't just win it, they looked like they could score every time they went forward. Their playmaker pulled the strings with a couple of assists, and the two players either side of him were both directly involved in goals as well. If you're a fan of this club, that performance should have you very excited — genuinely one of the most fun attacking sides to watch in the competition right now, and Serie A is wide open beyond the top one or two teams, so don't be shocked if they're still relevant come spring.

And to close it out — Lens against Slavia Praha was an absolute rollercoaster. Slavia were down to ten men from early in the second half, somehow still led 2-1 deep into stoppage time, and then Lens scored twice in the space of about three minutes to steal it 3-2. Brutal way to lose a game you'd more or less won.

## Sign-off

So that's Matchday 1 in the books. Not a ton to read into in the table yet — the hat-trick heroes are obviously top of the charts already, and keep an eye on the assist column too, because a couple of names are already pulling away there. Bigger picture: Haaland's on a pace that would break every record in this competition if he somehow kept it up, Barcelona look scarily complete, and there's already been more late drama than an entire group stage used to produce back in the old format.

That's it for the opening round. Thanks for watching, as always — weekend upload incoming.
